# Known Issues

Thanks for playing! If something here bothers you, or you find something new, open an [issue on GitHub](https://github.com/CBOC-Vita-Lab/galaxy-vita/issues).

## Multiplayer (3.0)

**Multiplayer has been verified live on a real PS Vita** (25–27 November 2026): Play Together on Wi-Fi with a second player joining from a computer (5–16 ms ping). Menus, gathering screen, fly-in, play, bubble rescue, checkpoints, even 60/30 pacing, smooth remote movement, full Luigi face-first fly-in, bubble-follow camera, and leaving all passed on hardware. Everything else (two physical Vitas together, Nearby ad hoc, far-away codes on real routers, four players on hardware) has been tried only on computers.
The checklist for additional console tests is in [docs/MULTIPLAYER-HARDWARE-TEST.md](docs/MULTIPLAYER-HARDWARE-TEST.md). Expect rough edges in untested paths, and please report them.

### Open: things you may run into
- **Heavy open galaxies in 2-player mode step down to a locked even 30 fps.** Both consoles must simulate the exact same world, so single player's far-object CPU shortcuts are off in multiplayer. Compact galaxies like Flipswitch hold a locked **60.0 fps**, while busy open galaxies like Good Egg step cleanly into a **locked even 30 fps** (zero uneven 50 fps judder). Remote player movement is smoothed with velocity-matched glide and 30 fps puppet animation cadence (verified on PS Vita).
- **The gathering screen, Luigi face-first fly-in, fly-in pushing, and bubble follow camera** are all fixed and hardware-verified on PS Vita.
- **Group-restart races and silent-link reconnects (5 to 70 seconds):** hardened in code (`MpWorldSync` / `MpFlow` epoch guards, unicast+broadcast `MP_PROBE`, direct `m_rejoinAddr` fallback, host-migration preemption, and reconnect state repair; host-verified in `restart_sync_test`, `world_test`, and `reconnect_probe_test`; two-console hardware verification pending).
- **The payload fuzz test (`tests/mp/payload_fuzz_build.sh`):** passing under ASan+UBSan on the release tree.
- **Boss fights are not shared.** Each console fights its own copy of the boss. Your hits do not hurt a friend's boss. The first player to win gets the star and the star ends the fight for everyone, so a player in the middle of a fight is taken to the star celebration. (Shared damage is possible later work.)
- **After a Star Race the leader decides what happens next, with one timer.** Only the leader sees **Race Again**, **Next Star** and **Leave Race**; a guest sees **Race Again?** (asks the leader, once per card) and **Leave Race**. If nobody touches the leader's card for 90 seconds, the game starts **Race Again** by itself, twice at most; the third time it chooses **Leave Race**. A press on the leader's card, or a guest's ask, starts the count again. It is a hidden timer, kept so that a leader who walked away cannot hold the others.
- **You cannot take a face off the gathering screen** before **START**. Every console on the Wi-Fi that is on that screen for the same mode shows up. Leave with **Circle** and start again, or use **Remove from Game** once the game is going.
- **A friend's page shows "Play with This Friend" only while that friend is in a Play with Anyone game.** Games made on the gathering screen (Wi-Fi, nearby, a code) are not visible to the service, so friends find each other with a code.
- **The leader has no switch for typed messages,** and the falls question cannot be changed after the first time (see [Multiplayer](docs/MULTIPLAYER.md#bubbles-and-the-falls-rule)). Each player can hide another player's messages.
- **Hide Messages is for the session only.** It follows the player, even into another place, but it is forgotten when the session ends.
- **Blocked Players lists only the players you blocked since the game last started.** Earlier blocks still hold on the service; **By code** unblocks one.
- **A race waits 3 minutes** after the first player finishes (it is fixed: the old setting for it has no screen).
- **If a player's connection freezes,** the game tries to put the player back for 30 to 75 seconds (see [Multiplayer](docs/MULTIPLAYER.md#if-you-drop-out)). Only computers have shown this. In a busy game with three players, a long freeze can still drop the player.
- **German and Italian have not been read by native speakers.** Wording may be odd.
- **The first screen after the title** (Single Player or Multiplayer) is an extra press for everyone. The game remembers your choice. Because of it, the round two-player button on the file select is hidden (see below).
- **A far-away code needs a router that allows it.** Some routers, mobile hotspots and shared networks block direct connections; the online relay covers strict NATs when enabled, or let your friend host instead.
- **Only Star Race times go to leaderboards.** Play Together times are not sent.
- **Remove from Game is not a permanent ban.** It keeps that console out of this game, including after the leader changes, for as long as the console keeps the identity it joined with (the saved key and the session token). A console whose settings were wiped, or a different console, is a new player and can come back. The game forgets the oldest of eight removed players when a ninth is removed, and the list ends with the game.
- **A returning player's place is held, so a newcomer can be told the game is full.** When a player's connection drops, the leader keeps that place for the returning console alone (up to 85 seconds after its last word). Someone else who tries to join meanwhile is told the game is full, and the game cannot tell them it is only for now.
- **On a single-player file, a friend's star can be refused, and you are told.** With **Play This File**, another player's star counts only once it has appeared in your own copy of the galaxy; the game waits 3 seconds for it. A hidden star, a boss star or a grand star can never appear on your console by itself (every console runs its own boss and hidden spots), so the game accepts those when they belong to the scenario you are playing and the other player is in the same scene as you. When a star is finally not saved, both players see "<name>'s Power Star couldn't be saved this time. Sorry!". A player who cheats could therefore credit your file with a star of the scenario you are in (not of another galaxy). The rules for a second star during your own celebration, a star the game does not record, and waiting for a loading player have only been checked on a computer, never in a real game.

### What multiplayer changes in single player
Even if you never play multiplayer, the 3.0 code is in the game. Written from an audit of the changes in the game's own files; nothing here has been measured on a console.
- **Your single-player save is written carefully (Safe saves is always on).** There is no row for it in Settings any more. Each save goes to a temporary file, is checked, and is then swapped in, so a power cut cannot leave half a file. You will see two side files next to `GameData.bin`: `GameData.bin.mpbak` (the save before, about 48 KiB) and, for a moment while saving (or after a power cut), `GameData.bin.mptmp`. A save takes a few milliseconds longer on a computer. It is always used while a multiplayer session is active.
- **The file select screen looks a little different.** The round two-player button is hidden (multiplayer is the second choice on the first screen after the title) and its pointing balloon says so. `SMG_MP_MENU=0` in `env.txt` switches the multiplayer menus off and brings the button back; that is a tester switch, not a way to reach Settings. With the multiplayer menus off there is no Single Player / Multiplayer screen, so Settings stays reachable from the pause menu (**△ Settings**) in single player.
- **Loading a save does a few small extra checks.** They make sure the numbers in the file are in range, and they only turn away a file that is malformed. A normal file loads as before.
- **At every start** the game makes the multiplayer screens' objects, reads one small file and looks at two others, and on a Vita sets up the system's dialogs. Nothing was measured on a console.
- **While you play,** nothing multiplayer runs each frame except a tiny check that returns at once when no multiplayer game is on, and a small check on the file select screen.
- **Two small fixes also change single player in rare cases:** a game file that had finished loading but was no longer wanted is now loaded again if it is asked for, and a wait on a task name that does not exist now returns instead of reading past the end.

### Not yet verified on a real console
Nobody has seen any of these work on a Vita. They are written from the code and from computer tests.
- **Two Vitas together.** Multiplayer has run on one real PS Vita (Play Together on Wi-Fi, menus, gathering, play, checkpoints, leaving) with the second player joining from a computer; a game between two Vitas has not been tried yet.
- **Reconnecting after a Wi-Fi drop, and coming back from sleep.** The 30 to 75 second return has run only against a cut link on a computer.
- **Wi-Fi keep-awake.** The game sends a small note at least every 50 ms so the Wi-Fi does not sleep. Only a simulation has run it.
- **Four players.** The frame rate and the memory with four players (and in heavy galaxies, and over hours) are not measured.
- **Shaders for other players' models:** precompiled into the 5,401-shader native PS Vita seed pack (including puppets, bubbles, 3D heads and multiplayer menus). If a rare costume/effect triggers an unseen shader, it compiles once and is cached on the memory card.
- **Nearby (ad hoc) play.** It has not been tried on real consoles at all.
- **Encrypted play on real networks.** What players send each other is encrypted and checked. That has run only on a computer, and the speed of the encryption on the Vita is an estimate.
- **The on-screen keyboard and the system's dialogs** (typing a friend's code, typed messages, the network checks).
- **Saves across a power cut.** On a computer, power was cut at many points of the multiplayer saves, the safe single-player save and the copies, and no file was left damaged. What a cut does on a Vita is not known.
- **What the safe single-player save costs on a Vita.** On a computer it takes 4.5 to 6 milliseconds a save instead of 1 to 2; on a Vita it is not measured.
- **Play This File** (saving into your single-player file) has only been tried by unit tests and computer runs, never in a game on a console.
- **The recovery question** after a crash ("Keep its progress?") has only been tried by unit tests, never in a game.
- **Safe Copies** (the screens, and the copy made when a new version starts) have only been run on a computer.
- **A far-away code on real routers.** Only pretend routers on a computer have been used.
- **How much the multiplayer code costs in single player.** See "What multiplayer changes in single player" below. Nobody has measured the cost on a console.

### The online service
- **Online play is live on `https://galaxy.psvita.dev`** (Cloudflare Worker custom domain with WAF custom rule, shared rate-limiting rule, Bot Fight Mode OFF, `workers.dev` disabled, and Cloudflare TURN relay + hard monthly egress caps active). Games on Wi-Fi, Nearby, and with a local address don't need it. Live console-to-console online play over the internet is brand-new at launch; please report any connection or NAT issues with `log.txt`.
- **Online play costs the service about 60 requests per player-hour,** so its daily allowance covers about 1,000 player-hours (a figure from computer tests, not from a live service). Wrong room codes are limited per network address (ten in 10 minutes, 40 a day), names are checked with one shared list, and an invite does not give out an address until the leader's console has accepted the joiner (it accepts by itself today, so that stops a code guesser who only reads, not one who joins).
- **When many people play, online play says "busy".** The service keeps a daily allowance. New games stop at about 80 percent of it, games already going continue, and the game stops asking for up to 10 minutes. The day starts over at 00:00 UTC. New accounts are limited to 10,000 a day in launch week and 2,000 afterwards. See [Multiplayer](docs/MULTIPLAYER.md#when-online-play-is-busy).
- **Leaderboards cannot tell a real race from two accounts of one person.** The service checks races (a second player on another network, with an account at least two days old, must send a matching time), and hides a time that looks too good. That makes cheating harder, not impossible.
- **Making an account can be made hard for everyone** if someone makes thousands of accounts from many addresses at once. The service limits sign-ups each day, asks every console for a small puzzle and tightens both as it fills.
- **The relay for players who cannot connect directly is optional and may be switched off on a service.** Without it, some pairs of players (for example behind strict routers) cannot play together online.

### Safety
- **Playing with strangers.** What players send each other once they have joined is encrypted and checked. But someone on the same Wi-Fi, or in radio range, can still jam a game, list fake games, or answer a join before the real game does so that a friend lands in a fake game. There is no password: anyone who can reach a game can join a free place and will receive the progress of the player who opened it, and the gathering screen puts together every console on the Wi-Fi that is on it. A removed player stays out only while the console keeps its identity (a console with wiped settings is new), and removing someone does not change the game's secret. Typed messages are not filtered (names are, with a shared list). Only play with people you know. See [Playing with strangers](docs/MULTIPLAYER.md#playing-with-strangers).
- **A far-away code is a key.** Anyone who has it can ask to join, and may learn your public address. Give it to one friend.
- **Saves.** Outside reviews found ways multiplayer could change your single-player file, or fail to make a backup first. They have been fixed and tested on a computer, and nobody has tried them on a console. Until a console test has passed, **copy `ux0:data/smg/saves` to your computer before you play multiplayer**, and be careful with **Play This File**.
- **Recovery checkpoints are checked before they are offered.** A blank checkpoint is never offered, and one with fewer Power Stars than the file now has (when the file was saved later) is set aside, not offered. Putting a checkpoint into a file that has more stars or is newer asks first. These are the fixes for the one random computer test that ended with a blank single-player file; the cause was found, but none of it has run on a console.
- **The ordinary single-player save goes through a temporary file (always on).** This protects against a power cut in the middle of a save. You will see a `GameData.bin.mpbak` (about 48 KiB) beside your `GameData.bin`, and sometimes a `.mptmp` for a moment. It costs a few milliseconds a save on a computer; on a Vita it is not measured. Testers can switch it off with `SMG_ATOMIC_SAVES=0` in `ux0:data/smg/env.txt` ([Tester switches](docs/TESTER-SWITCHES.md)). **Caveats:** a missing `GameData.bin` is put back at the next start only when a `.mptmp` is beside it (a save cut in the middle); a lone `.mpbak` is never restored, so a save deleted by hand stays deleted. After **Play This File** with Safe saves forced off by a tester, the `.mpbak` that session made is removed again. An older version ignores the extra files.
- **Safe Copies exist, but nobody has used them on a console.** A new version makes one checked copy of your multiplayer data when it starts; **Safe Copies** makes and brings back copies. You find it under **How to Play** (**Triangle** on the Multiplayer menu, **OK**, then **Yes** on the second page) or with **Triangle** on **Which File?**; **Bring Back a Copy** is also offered where a file cannot be read, once a copy exists. The copy holds your online sign-in (your Friend Code) unless you choose **Make a Copy to Share**. If the card does not answer within 15 seconds the game stops waiting and says to try again; nothing was changed.

## Performance
- **Honeyhive and Beach Bowl at the widest camera zoom (1.0×)** are the heaviest scenes for the Vita. Quality (Picture > Full Detail) steps resolution down dynamically when needed to keep 30 fps with native HUD. The closer default zoom (1.5×) stays sharper there.
- **Performance mode (Settings > Picture > Smooth Motion) targets 60 fps at full 960×544 almost everywhere** (clean 2-mode Settings UX: 60 fps Performance and 30 fps Quality; the user-facing 50 fps toggle is retired, while `GXV_HRW_ALLOW50=1` remains as an `env.txt` escape hatch). Brief spikes no longer snap-drop or trap a stage at 30 fps: dropping to an even 30 requires two consecutive 5-second quiet windows below 52 fps, recovery trials back off at most `10 -> 20 -> 30 s`, and quiet measured headroom triggers a fast 3-second recovery trial. Verified on PS Vita (Honeyhive 59.9 fps native, Melty Molten 55–59.6 fps, Dreadnought 55–57 fps with clean even-30 fallback). Add `GXV_PERF_BUDGET=0` to `ux0:data/smg/env.txt` for full far detail.
- **Rare unseeded shader compiles in play:** the package ships a complete **5,401-shader native PS Vita seed pack** covering all 134 Galaxy:Scenario stops, 18 bosses, all 7 power-ups, multiplayer menus/puppets, and Settings (0 cold compiles observed in cold-boot testing across Title, Observatory, Good Egg, Honeyhive, Beach Bowl, Melty Molten, and Dreadnought). If an unusual state triggers a new shader, your memory card caches it once so it never compiles again.
- **Quality mode (Settings > Picture > Full Detail) targets locked 30 fps at native 960×544 with hardware 2× MSAA** (`GXV_Q30_MSAA=1`, `GXV_Q30_MSAA_STENCIL=1`), trilinear + hardware anisotropic auto-mipmaps, sharp character LOD (`GXV_PLAYER_LODBIAS=-50`, `GXV_PLAYER_ANISO=3`), and full hardware AF preserved on blended/water/decal surfaces. Verified on PS Vita (Dreadnought and Melty Molten locked 30.0 fps at `960×544 2× MSAA`, Beach Bowl 28.6–30.2 fps). Under sustained load, Quality automatically steps down from `2× MSAA` to `1×` native before any detail cut (`GXV_Q30_MSAA=0` in `env.txt` pins `1×`).
- **Without the CapUnlocker plug-in** the game can't use the Vita's fourth CPU core, and the busiest scenes run a few fps slower.
- **Loading screen and Settings background** use your own title screen (from your second start on). Delete `ux0:data/smg/art/title.rgba` to capture it again, or add `GXV_TITLEART=0` to `ux0:data/smg/env.txt` to switch it off.

## Gameplay
- **Surfing and the Star Ball steer by tilting the Vita, and a few consoles have a motion sensor that doesn't work.** Since 2.3 the game switches those levels to the left stick by itself when the sensor's readings stay frozen while you move the stick (about 4 seconds) or it doesn't answer a fully pushed stick for 1.5 seconds; `log.txt` shows a `tilt:` line when it does, and your saved setting is not changed. To choose the stick yourself and skip the wait: open **Settings** from the pause menu (**△ Settings**) or from the Single Player / Multiplayer screen, go to **Controls**, set **Tilt** to **Left stick**, and adjust **Tilt Strength** 1 to 10 if you like (it applies at once). To switch the automatic part off, add `GXV_TILT_AUTOSTICK=0` to `ux0:data/smg/env.txt`. This has not yet been tried on a console with a dead sensor.
- **If a power-up can't be used, it stays where it is.** If the models for a form (Bee, Ice, Spring, Boo, Rainbow) aren't loaded, touching the item does nothing instead of using it up, and `log.txt` says `player-assets:`. If you see this in a galaxy that should have the form, please report it with `log.txt`.
- **Rear touch is off by default**, so fingers holding the Vita never move the pointer by accident. Turn it on in Settings > Controls > Rear Touch (pause menu, △ Settings).
- **A rare brief pause the first time an unseen effect appears.** The package ships 5,401 precompiled shaders in memory (`~0.4 s` preload at start-up); anything rare it doesn't have is prepared once and kept on your memory card.
- **Kitchen dome crash:** 2.3 fixes the fault we believe caused it, but it hasn't been confirmed. If the game still closes there, please send `log.txt` and the crash report (see below).

## Install
- The game needs your own `RMGE01` (Wii, USA) disc image. Other regions aren't supported yet.
- `libshacccg.suprx` must be set up on the Vita once (the installer and [Install](MANUAL-INSTALL.md) explain how).
- **The LiveArea still shows the old look or the old Settings tile after updating:** the Vita doesn't refresh a game's LiveArea when you install over it. The Settings tile is gone from the package; Settings are in the game (**Settings** on the Single Player / Multiplayer screen, **△ Settings** in the pause menu). The old tile stays until you reinstall the game (or use VitaShell's Refresh LiveArea); tapping it opens the in-game Settings. Your saves are in `ux0:data/smg` and stay.
- **Controller plugins & "Controller disconnected" popups (fixed in 3.0.1):** Handheld Vitas running controller plugins such as `MiniVitaTV`, `ds4vita`, `reVita`, or third-party Bluetooth pad plugins now fail over cleanly across all active ports and plain `sceCtrlPeekBufferPositive(0)` fallback without triggering false controller-disconnect dialogs or misclassifying handhelds as PS TV. Genuine unpaired PS TV disconnect detection is preserved.
- **Fan-translated / patched ISOs (fixed in 3.0.1):** Replacing game files over an existing install now automatically invalidates `sizes.cache` (`fst.bin`, `Message.arc`, `Font.arc`), and malformed `0x1A` text tags in custom translations are safely guarded.
- **Sleep / LiveArea resume (fixed in 3.0.1):** CPU/GPU/bus clocks (`GXV_CLOCK_KEEP=1` by default) and pad/touch/motion sampling now refresh immediately upon waking from sleep or returning from LiveArea.
- **Preparing the game by hand needs Python 3** on your computer, and shrinking the movies by hand (optional, to save card space) needs a C++ compiler. The full-size movies play smoothly; follow step 3 of [Prepare Your Game](MANUAL-PREPARE-GAME.md) only if you want the smaller ones. The installer does both for you.

## HD Textures
- **The HD pack is optional and is not part of the download.** It is made from your own game files (about 10 to 30 minutes on a computer), so the project doesn't host it. The steps are in [HD textures](tools/hdtex/README.md): build it, copy `hdtex.pack` to `ux0:data/smg/hdtex.pack`, and the game finds it at the next start. A pack someone else hosts is not from this project and we can't vouch for it. Updating the game or the installer never touches your `hdtex.pack`.
- **What it changes:** about 90 textures of Mario, Luigi, the Bee, Boo and other forms, Rosalina, Peach, Bowser and key items are drawn from a 2x image. It is most visible in close-ups, cutscenes and the Observatory; at normal play distance (Mario is about 90 px tall) most of the gain is not resolved by the screen. The rest of the game is unchanged.
- **What it costs:** about 4.5 MiB of the pack's size in main memory, and about 0.3 to 1.6 MiB of video memory in a scene (about 3x what the original images of those textures take, the Bowser fight most). Each texture is uploaded once when it first appears, so a scene's first moments can cost a little. The pack is not expected to change the frame rate noticeably; if you see a drop, compare with `GXV_HDTEX=0`.
- **Check that it is in use:** `log.txt` has an `hdtex: pack ... found` line at start and an `hdtex: scene ...` line per scene. If it says `no pack`, the file is not at `ux0:data/smg/hdtex.pack`.

## If Something Goes Wrong
- **Something looks or runs wrong:** the game keeps a short log in `ux0:data/smg/log.txt` (and the session before it in `log.txt.prev`): the frame rate and any stutters every few seconds, picture changes, settings and errors. Attach it to your [issue](https://github.com/CBOC-Vita-Lab/galaxy-vita/issues).
- **The game closed by itself:** since 2.3 the last lines of `log.txt` say why, and the Vita saves a crash report as `ux0:data/psp2core-….psp2dmp`. Attach both to your issue.
- **The picture keeps changing sharpness, or you want it fixed:** add `GXV_AUTOQ=0` to `ux0:data/smg/env.txt` to keep the standard picture.
- **The HD character textures don't look right:** delete `ux0:data/smg/hdtex.pack`, or add `GXV_HDTEX=0` to the same file, to use the originals. The game runs the same without the pack.
